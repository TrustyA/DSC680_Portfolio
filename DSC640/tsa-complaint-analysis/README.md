Overview

This project explores patterns in TSA complaints across different airports and categories. The goal was to use visualization techniques to better understand where and why complaints are occurring, and to identify any trends that could point to operational or customer experience issues.

Objective

The objective was to analyze complaint data and create clear visualizations that highlight trends over time, differences between airports, and the most common types of complaints. The project focuses on turning raw data into insights that are easy to understand.

Data

The dataset consists of TSA complaint records, including complaint categories, airport codes, and timestamps. Additional data was used to map airport codes to full airport names for clearer interpretation.

Tools & Technologies

Python, Jupyter Notebook, pandas, matplotlib, seaborn (and/or R depending on version used)

Methodology

The data was cleaned and prepared by standardizing column names, handling missing values, and merging datasets where needed. Time-based features were created to analyze trends over months and seasons.

Visualizations were used as the primary method of analysis, including bar charts, time series plots, and category comparisons. These helped highlight which complaint types were most frequent and how they varied across locations.

Results

The analysis revealed that certain complaint categories appear more frequently than others, with noticeable variation depending on the airport and time period. Some seasonal trends were also observed, suggesting that travel volume and timing may influence complaint frequency.

Key Takeaways

This project demonstrates the ability to clean and combine datasets, perform exploratory data analysis, and communicate findings through visualizations. It also highlights how data can be used to better understand customer experience and identify areas for improvement.
